01 / Overview

The Injury

Articular fractures of the proximal tibia across a wide severity range — stable low-energy split-depressions at one end, limb-threatening bicondylar and shaft-dissociated patterns at the other.

Population A

Young · High-energy

MVC, falls from height, sport. Comminuted, often bicondylar.

Population B

Elderly · Low-energy

Simple falls on osteopenic bone. Fragile skin and poor bone quality drive the dilemma.

Mechanism. Valgus + axial load → lateral split / split-depression (needs an intact MCL as a hinge). Varus → medial failure. Axial-predominant → bicondylar. Varus-hyperextension → posterior tension + anterior compression with slope reversal (Firoozabadi).

Associated injury. Ligament injury up to 71%; a knee-dislocation event in 46% of Schatzker IV. Rim fractures (Segond, reverse Segond, anteromedial margin, semimembranosus) ≈ cruciate injury. Screen every case: serial compartment exams (Schatzker IV & VI highest risk), ABI < 0.9 → CT angiogram, and read the skin — a positive wrinkle sign signals surgical readiness.

Imaging. AP / lateral + a 10–15° caudal plateau view; CT is routine (fragment map, depression, comminution — 3D often changes the plan); MRI is most sensitive for meniscal / ligament and occult fractures.

02 / Anatomy

How It's Built

Lateral plateau — flat/convex, higher, more meniscal coverage, fails more readily. Medial plateau — concave, congruent, more resistant. Posterior slope ~5° medial / ~7.5° lateral (Ricci): template off the uninjured side. Landmarks: Gerdy tubercle (IT band) laterally, pes tendons anteromedially, the fibula buttressing the lateral plateau.

Neurovascular. The common peroneal nerve wraps the fibular neck (at risk in varus medial patterns & posterolateral exposure); the popliteal artery trifurcation lies where displacement occurs; the anterior tibial artery is tethered at the interosseous membrane and endangered in shaft-dissociated patterns.

Fluoroscopy pearlBecause the medial plateau is concave and the lateral convex, the two can be assessed separately on a true lateral — the basis for reading reduction intra-operatively.

The Three-Column Concept · Luo

On axial CT the plateau splits into lateral, medial and posterior columns. A purely medial/lateral plate fails to buttress a posterior fragment — which is why posterior-shear patterns fail secondarily.

04 / Management

The Plan

01 — Nonoperative

When to leave it alone

Indications
  • Undisplaced / minimally displaced.
  • Small lateral depression, no instability.
  • High comorbidity; low-demand elderly.
Avoid if
  • Knee instability.
  • Predictable deformity.
  • Any displaced medial-side injury.
PearlA fixed millimetre cut-off is too simplistic — measurement is unreliable and pattern-specific deformity risk matters more. Localised depressions up to ~10 mm can still give a stable, functional knee.
02 — Operative principles

Reduce · Align · Move

  • Goal: stable articular reduction + restored alignment + early motion.
  • Lateral surgical triggers: split, >½-surface depression, fibular-head fracture, valgus instability.
  • Young patients: nearly all bicondylar & shaft-dissociated patterns are operative.
  • Visualise the joint by fluoro, arthroscopy, or submeniscal arthrotomy.
03 — Staging & ex-fix

Respect the envelope

Stage it
  • Complex bicondylar — spanning ex-fix restores length/alignment, protects soft tissue before ORIF. Pins clear of plate sites.
Skip / definitive
  • Schatzker I–III are length-stable — no ex-fix unless open.
  • Fine-wire/ring for severe soft-tissue injury; matches locked plating with fewer complications.
Early total care?Fixation <48–72 h shows comparable rates only with healthy soft tissues, a positive wrinkle sign, and lower-energy mechanisms.
04 — Fixation & void

Buttress · Raft · Antiglide

  • Lateral split-depression: buttress plate + subchondral rafting screws.
  • Posteromedial shear: antiglide plate at the fragment apex — a lateral plate alone leaves it unbuttressed → reduction loss.
  • Bicondylar: fix the smaller/undisplaced condyle first, then the other with a locking construct; dual plating for coronal fragments.
Void fillingCalcium-/tricalcium-phosphate resists depression well — one β-TCP series reported 0% loss of reduction.
